## Audit basis

I reviewed the revised TeX, `harness/run.py`, `harness/verify_task.py`, the T004-T054 task files, the analysis code and CSVs, `refs/notes.md`, and the frozen `data/results_snapshot.db`. I did not query the live database. The snapshot has 655 total runs, of which 637 are ladder attempts. Direct SQLite queries with `error IS NULL AND turns IS NOT NULL` give 552 valid ladder runs. Excluding the 21 run IDs marked escaped in `analysis/out/escape_runs.csv` leaves 531 NoEsc runs. Direct queries give planted-function drift of 33/56 and 70/125 on the two loose rungs before exclusions, with 102/103 drifters passing hidden tests. The corresponding NoEsc counts are 31/52 and 69/117, with 99/100 drifters passing hidden tests. These agree with the CSVs and macros.

I ran `python3 harness/verify_task.py`: all 49 task directories that ship `_solution/` passed its three states. A separate byte comparison found the `repo/` and `tests/` trees identical across each domain's three rungs. All 51 metadata files declare two visible tests, six hidden tests, and four rabbit holes. The test command occurs in 17/17 scoped, 1/17 loose, and 0/17 vague prompts. All 36 cited keys occur in both `refs/notes.md` and the bibliography; the semantic citation problems below remain.

The sentence that assigns 14 escapes to Gemini and seven to Grok is correct (`escape_runs.csv`). The complete-coverage zero-loose-drift arms are Opus 4.8 and Opus 5.5, one in each generation. "Scoped runs made none" is correct only under NoEsc for the broad measure. Among NoEsc vague broad-edit runs, test files were changed in 7/7 and 10/15; among NoEsc loose broad-edit runs, non-test out-of-scope changes occurred in 31/35 and 76/81. These counts support "mostly tests" and "mostly code" only as edit categories, not as a claim about executable statements.

I built a scratch copy with `./build.sh`, keeping the analyzer's repository root pointed at the original task and run artifacts so its escape audit used the same paths. The regenerated `macros.tex` and `_paper_macros.tex` match the supplied files byte for byte, and the rebuilt PDF has the same extracted text as `main.pdf`. I rendered all 20 pages with `pdftoppm -r 70`. Captions are above tables and below figures. I found no red `[Macro]` marker, `??` reference, multiply defined label, or undefined citation in the PDF or log. The log does report a 4.06 pt overfull line at `sections/introduction.tex:68-73`.

## New problems introduced by the revision

1. **High: the new all-runs robustness sentence is false for the new broad outcome.** `sections/results.tex:10-13` says that including escaped runs "changes no conclusion below". Table 3 and `scope_generation.csv` show NoEsc scoped broad edits at 0/51 and 0/132, but all-valid generation-1 scoped edits at **3/56**. Runs 16, 102, and 127 edited visible tests and were all excluded as escaped. Thus `sections/results.tex:95-102` and the conclusion's scoped-versus-vague broad contrast depend on the exclusion. **Fix:** state that the planted-function headline is stable under inclusion, but the broad scoped-zero claim is not; give both populations' counts in the sensitivity sentence.

2. **High: the revised paper now measures vague-prompt scope violations but still says vagueness does not move agents out of scope.** `sections/abstract.tex:15-20` itself reports vague broad edits in about an eighth of runs, and Table 3 gives **7/54 (13.0%)** and **15/125 (12.0%)** versus zero scoped broad edits. Yet `sections/introduction.tex:77-80`, `sections/discussion.tex:21-30`, `sections/related.tex:91-114`, and `sections/conclusion.tex:12-15` say missing detail does not drive out-of-scope action. The title has the same unqualified message. This conflates the planted-function measure with the new broader measure. **Fix:** make every such claim explicitly about the four planted functions, and state that vague requests did produce other scope violations, primarily test edits. Avoid turning an observed difference into a universal claim about vagueness.

3. **High: the frozen database alone cannot reproduce the new headline table.** `sections/introduction.tex:85-87` and `sections/statements.tex:12-16,25-27` say every number and table comes from the results database. The revised `analysis/audit_analysis.py:19-36` also reads trajectories and final worktrees. `analysis/scope_audit.py:162-179` locates them through `run.trajectory_path`, which is stored as an absolute `/Users/psudokit/benchmark/runs/...` path in the snapshot. The new test-edit, broad-scope, exposure, and escape results are absent from the SQLite schema. A copied paper tree cannot regenerate them at a new path without remapping and the run files. **Fix:** archive the exact run worktrees and trajectories as part of the frozen release, store or remap relative artifact paths, verify their hashes, and amend the reproducibility statement to name every input.

4. **Medium: the last results paragraph silently switches from NoEsc back to all-valid rates.** `sections/results.tex:11-13` establishes NoEsc as the default. Yet `sections/results.tex:150-162` uses `\SuiteGtwoCoreDriftPct` and the `\CrossGrok...` macros, which come from all-valid `suite_loose.csv` and `cross_generation.csv` (`analysis/analyze.py:410-433,680-687`). The printed core rate is **24/55 (43.6%)**, while NoEsc is **24/48 (50.0%)**; the printed algo rate is 30/42 (71.4%) versus NoEsc 29/41 (70.7%). The Grok successor comparison uses 4/7 and 1/7, while the NoEsc shared loose cells have 4/5 and 1/4. Figure 5 also plots all-valid domain rates without saying so (`sections/appendix.tex:25-29`). **Fix:** recompute the paragraph and figure for NoEsc or label the population at the start of the paragraph and in the figure caption, with a NoEsc sensitivity comparison.

5. **Medium: a file-read audit is presented as evidence that agents saw the planted functions.** `sections/results.tex:70-84` says vague agents "read the bait" and "read them". `analysis/trajectory_audit.py:13` defines exposure as a successful read of a file containing a rabbit-hole symbol, excluding listings and edits, but it does not require the function's lines to appear in output. The 49/54 and 116/125 counts support file exposure, not function exposure or an exposure mechanism. The claim about "the same agents" also lacks paired evidence in the text; escaped counterparts remove some paired cells. **Fix:** say that vague runs opened non-target files containing planted functions, and reserve function-exposure claims for line-level read evidence.

6. **Medium: "any out-of-scope" is stronger than the new detector.** `sections/method.tex:73-79` and the Table 3 caption call the union "any out-of-scope edit". `analysis/scope_audit.py:23-35,113-136` deliberately ignores edits to the target function's docstring and imports, plus permissions and empty directories. The task contract forbids docstring edits (`tasks/T004-percentile-nearest-rank/meta.yaml:17-22`), and `sections/limitations.tex:48-51` admits this blind spot. **Fix:** either add those contract violations to the detector or rename the union to "measured out-of-scope edit" and enumerate its exclusions beside the table.

7. **Medium: the revised literature synthesis claims a failure mode the experiment did not measure.** `sections/related.tex:10-16` says Amodei et al. "name two of the failure modes we measure" and then calls reward hacking one of them. `refs/notes.md:140-143` defines reward hacking as gaming the reward. The observed test-file edits and planted edits do not establish that agents optimized or gamed the test oracle. The same paragraph has a grammatical break: "Their negative side effects, where ... , is close to what our rabbit holes probe". **Fix:** call reward hacking background for the visible/hidden design and negative side effects the analogy for unrequested edits. Rewrite the latter as two short sentences, for example: "Negative side effects are the closer analogy. Visible tests do not check the deduplication helper, so an agent can rewrite it while passing them."

8. **Low: the new outcomes table appears before its section.** At 70 dpi, Table 3 and its caption occupy the top of rendered page 7, followed by the end of Experimental setup; the Results heading starts below them. The source introduces Table 3 only in `sections/results.tex:20-42`, after the heading. **Fix:** place a float barrier before Results or make this table nonfloating so the heading precedes the outcomes.

9. **Low: the new coverage caption says escaped runs read files, although the audit includes attempted access and directory listing.** `sections/appendix.tex:13-16` calls escaped runs those "whose trajectory reads files outside the working copy". `analysis/trajectory_audit.py:13` also counts listing/searching an ancestor, attempted reads of missing paths, and commands whose targets resolve outside the worktree. `macros.tex` records 11 ancestor-only escapes. **Fix:** say "attempted to read, list, or search benchmark files outside the working copy".

## Writing and presentation pass

The following revised sentences still sound over-polished or assert more than the evidence allows. Suggested rewrites use no em dashes.

- `sections/discussion.tex:21-23`: "Here, missing information made agents work harder, not wander further." Rewrite: "Vague runs used more tool calls and made no planted-function edits. They did make other out-of-scope edits in 12-13% of NoEsc runs."
- `sections/related.tex:37-40`: "We look for something milder and, we suspect, far more common in daily use." The prevalence claim has no field estimate. Rewrite: "We measure unrequested edits during bug fixes, including edits that leave all tests passing."
- `sections/results.tex:83-84`: "The same agents that rewrote all four rabbit holes under the loose prompt read them under the vague prompt and left them alone." The exposure audit is at file level, and some corresponding vague cells were excluded. Rewrite: "Vague runs often opened non-target files that contained planted functions, but none edited those functions."
- `sections/abstract.tex:18-20`: "Inviting cleanup, not leaving out detail, is what moves agents out of scope." Rewrite: "In this ladder, cleanup wording produced many more unrequested edits than either fix request; vague requests also produced some test edits."

The compact coverage table is readable, and tables and figures have correctly placed captions. Appendix C still needs its heading and figure kept together. The rebuilt log's small overfull line at `sections/introduction.tex:68-73` is visible but less urgent than the claim and population errors.

## Summary

The planted-function result survives the frozen NoEsc audit: 31/52 and 69/117 loose runs drifted, versus zero planted edits on scoped and vague runs. The revised broad outcome changes the interpretation: vague runs do make out-of-scope edits, escaped runs change the scoped broad zero, and incomplete sessions and nonportable run artifacts still limit the paper's strongest claims. Correct those claims and the lingering Appendix C layout before showing the paper.
